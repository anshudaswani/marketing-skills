# Genericness check

Could a competitor have published this?

If yes, send it back.

## How to run it

Take the output: a thesis, a positioning line, a headline, a post, a plan. Swap the company name for its nearest competitor's. Read it again.

If it still works, it says nothing. It is describing the category, not the company.

## What usually fails

- Claims about the category dressed as claims about the company ("AI is transforming advertising").
- Benefits every vendor promises (faster, smarter, at scale, in minutes).
- Positions that depend on an adjective (best, leading, modern, next-generation).
- Customer language the company has not earned from its own customers.
- A "so what" that recommends differentiating without saying on what.

## What passes

- A claim only this company can make because of its product, its customers, its founders, or its timing.
- A number from its own evidence.
- A sentence a competitor would have to disagree with.
- Something true that the rest of the category is choosing not to say.

## Output

Pass, or send back with one line on why and which of the failures above it hit.
