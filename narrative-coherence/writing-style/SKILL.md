# How AD writes

Built from published copy, captions, sent emails, and the way she talks in working sessions. A starting point she corrects over time. The structure is the reusable part: a through-line, hard rules, the words she reaches for, format notes, and what to avoid sounding like.

## The through-line

Short sentences. Plain words. One idea at a time, said once. She writes like a marketer talking to another marketer who is busy: no warm-up, no jargon, no hedging, and the point lands in the first line. She would rather sound direct than clever, but she likes a line that turns.

Her signature move is the contrast pair: two short sentences that set one thing against another.

- "Revenue moves last. Brand moves first."
- "You don't have a demand problem. You have a legibility problem."
- "Everything is automatable. But automation was never the real threat."

Use it, but not on every line. One per piece is a signature; three is a tic.

## Hard rules

- No em dashes, ever. Use a comma, a full stop, or a colon.
- No fabricated quotes. Quotes are verbatim and attributed; never stitch two together. A paraphrase runs without quotation marks.
- Never invent a number. A figure needs a source or it does not go in.
- Avoid "genuinely", "honestly", "leverage", "unlock", "delve", "game-changer", "agentic".
- Avoid "It's not X, it's Y" drama unless it is the one contrast pair in the piece.
- No bullet lists in prose she will publish. Bullets are fine in a working note.

## Words she reaches for

legible / legibility, coherence, foundation, infrastructure, the work beneath it, evidence, signal, the read, what moved, what landed, what it cost, the ask, the hook, the opener, sit with, lock, nudge, "before it hits pipeline".

She says "we" for the company and "I" for her own calls. She names owners plainly.

## By format

**Chat.** Lowercase, fast, no punctuation fuss. Match it; don't tidy it.

**Email.** Warm and brisk. One line of warmth, the ask in one sentence, out. A rescheduling note is three lines, not a paragraph.

**Outreach.** Lead with something true about their world that they can't get elsewhere. No pitch in the first email. End on a question that is easy to answer.

**Slide copy.** Clipped declaratives, fragments allowed. Titles as statements. Questions beat verdicts.

**Captions.** A one-line hook that states a tension, three or four short lines that earn it, a closing line that turns. One hashtag at most. No emojis.

**Essays.** Argument first, title as a reframe of a problem the reader thinks they have. Each paragraph carries one idea.

## What to avoid sounding like

- Consultant: "leverage cross-functional alignment to drive outcomes"
- Vendor: "our platform enables real-time competitive intelligence"
- Hype: "this changes everything"
- Over-hedged: "it might perhaps be worth considering"

If a sentence could appear in any company's deck, cut it or make it specific.

## Before handing over a draft

Read it aloud in her register: would she say this sentence to a colleague across a desk? If not, shorten it. Check for em dashes, invented numbers, and quotes that are not verbatim. Then check that the first line is the point.
